Julian Maciej Rybicki (2 November 1931 - 2 May 1987) was a Rosen political theorist, socialist, and dictator who ruled the [[First Rosen Commonwealth]] from 1983 to 1987. 
Julian Rybicki and his brother, Bartosz, entered the [[Socialist Party]] in 1969 as militants specializing in sabotage and disruption. He rapidly acquired power through manipulation, coercion, and blackmail until he led a coup against civilian and military party leadership in 1983, with the backing of prominent reformers including most of [[The Five Figures]]. Against the wishes of his base of support, he immediately began to consolidate power, turning the military leadership against the civilian leadership. He replaced the civilian Politburo with the powerless People's National Advisory Assembly (PNAA), and then appointed an Executive Committee (EXCOM) of yes-men to manage domestic policy.
In 1984, Rybicki and the EXCOM assembled a comprehensive economic overhaul program, called the New Society Economic Program (NSEP). This replaced the decentralized economy with a command economy, and called for the acquisition of "collaborator" corporations like Iseva and Hafiz Industries. The NSEP hinged on the reliability of Rybicki's cult of personality, which hit its apex at the time the Program was implemented. NSEP saw some minor successes in rural areas at redistributing land ownership, but as it continued, it became increasingly clear that NSEP was a litmus test of Rybicki's inner circle's loyalty.
In the wake of the corruption, starvation, and institutional failure that followed the height of NSEP in 1985, Rybicki faced overt rebellion from the moderates who put him in power, notably [[Jorge Almeida]] and Bartosz Rybicki. In response, Rybicki escalated the NSEP purges against his former base, disappearing Almeida and Bartosz and executing them in April. This was successful in putting down overt resistance, until the Party military leadership, specifically the naval branch, led a brief coup against the EXCOM and replaced them until the pro-Rybicki segment of the military led a counter-coup. 
Rybicki's leadership again faced challenges with the Wallaceburg attacks, which he claimed were perpetrated by [[ICOSGEA]]. Confidence in Rybicki declined as his health worsened, resulting in a heart attack in May 1987 and the end of his rule. He was replaced by an EXCOM collective presidency.
