The Great Consensus, also known as the Consensus, is a term referring to the period of relative peace, compromise, and agreement throughout the 2000s and 2010s. 
Following the [[Nuclear Crisis]], the [[International Union]] found itself in a position of supranational power due to its ability to effectively distribute aid across the planet, primarily at the expense of the countries of The Arawad. The Consensus saw the IU use its newfound power to broker longstanding peace agreements among major powers, especially the [[Rosen Commonwealth]], [[Saya]], and the [[West Ayan Republic]]. 
While the Consensus was not a formal agreement, many consider it to comprise several landmark treaties and agreements. These include the formation of the International Aid Establishment (IAE)(1998), the [[International Security Directory]] (ISD)(2000), the International Center for Disease Control (ICDC)(2005), the World Agricultural Distribution Fund (WADF)(2009), and the Comprehensive Trilateral Arms Control Agreement (CTACA)(2014). The IU greatly expanded its responsibilities and powers during this time, improving its capability to arbitrate disputes.
