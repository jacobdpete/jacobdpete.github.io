> [!Summary]
> In the [[Rosen Commonwealth]], marriage is available as a service provided by local religious authorities or courts. Marriage is defined by the Rosen Local Civics Code as a "long-term social commitment between one or more individuals." One must be eighteen or older to obtain a marriage certification. Because of the wide definition of marriage, many commentators assert that the Commonwealth has abolished marriage, primarily due to [[Jorge Almeida]] and [[Hatsue Yuhara]]'s social views.

# Ideology
Hatsue Yuhara and Bartłomiej Koppa were the primary contributors to the social aspects of [[Almeidaism]], with the former placing particular emphasis on marital abolition. In *Socialist-Feminist Liberation*, Yuhara's primary work outlining her feminist views in tandem with socialism, Yuhara asserted that marriage was largely a patriarchal institution that limited women and enriched the social standing of men. Abolishing marriage would in turn remove a formal obligation to unpaid domestic labor from women, a key concept Yuhara used to join the feminist idea of marital abolition to the leftist idea of the labor theory of value. Yuhara also claimed that the abolition of marriage would lead to stronger communities, as marriage creates indivisible and individualist units that only hinder the need for collective support.
Despite Yuhara's ample criticism of marriage, she did not provide significant direction for the process of abolishing marriage and what should replace it. Later speeches and letters indicated she was in support of libertarian-style customized contracts between familial units, with each contract fitting the needs of the specific unit. This practice has become somewhat commonplace among familial units that feel the need to formalize their arrangement, but the fairly casual nature of marriage and marriage-equivalents in the Commonwealth still means these customized contracts aren't the norm.
Jorge Almeida initially did not have particularly strong feelings on marital abolition, a disagreement between him and Yuhara that resulted in them severing ties briefly before his execution in 1985. Almeida explicitly stated this in his *April 1985 Letters*, saying: "I don't particularly care what \[Yuhara\] has to say about marriage, it's unimportant." Later letters in the collection indicated he had given some thought to the idea, and he eventually decided it fit into the *relationships principle*, noting: "commodified intimacy is invalid and thus the abolition of marriage shall decommodify."
# Legal mechanism
## History
The issue of marital abolition was not a high priority for the Commonwealth until well into the 2010s, when in 2017 Senator Marianna Krych proposed the Marital Abolition Bill (MAB), which would gradually invalidate existing marriages and replace them with contracts and informal agreements between those participating. This was widely unpopular with the Senate and with Secretary-General Giorno-Tulle's first [[Secretariat-Directory|Directory]]. Giorno-Tulle's Secretary of Health, Labor, and Welfare, Simon Kirkegaard, initiated with the consent of the Directory the Marital Redefinition Bill (MDB), which would instead vaguely define marriage rather than outright abolish it, protecting existing arrangements while opening the door for legally-protected non-traditional familial units. The MDB classified marriage as a "long-term social commitment" as opposed to a romantic commitment. It also protected marriages involving more than two participants by not strictly defining marriage to a number of participants, provided it was two or more. The MDB, indirectly, legally protected same-sex marriage, as ruled in Marcussen v. Correia.
As such, marriages can look like anything in the Commonwealth provided there are two or more participants, the participants involved can provide evidence they are committed to each other and any minors involved, and the participants are above the age of eighteen. 
## Process and obligations
Marriage certifications are provided at no cost by local courts. In municipalities, district courts provide certifications. In provinces, county courts provide certifications. 
The certification itself mandates that participants be committed to one another. This does not necessitate cohabitation or romantic interest, but it does obligate regular contact and the ability to function well as a unit. 
The presence of abuse, dysfunction, and a lack of regular contact are all grounds to reverse a certification[^1]. A certification can be reversed through mutual agreement of all participants involved in the unit or a ruling by a judge initiated by a community report.[^2]
Marriage certifications can be used to jointly file taxes, as proof of residence, or for other legal necessities. Some [[Republic|republics]] put marital status on IDs.
Marriage certifications, in some republics, can be provided, also at no cost, by local religious institutions. However, this process is almost entirely ceremonial, as a judge or a judge-appointed representative must still be present at the religious ceremony.
# Controversy
There is debate in the Commonwealth over whether marriage was truly abolished or repackaged.
Defenders of the status quo note that marriage is almost entirely ceremonial, and with no meaning to marriage, then there is no reason to formally abolish it. Formally abolishing marriage would be immensely complex and costly, which is why the MDB passed instead of the MAB; it had very little public support because it would invalidate millions fairly unproblematic marriages.
Opponents argue that redefining marriage to meaninglessness doesn't qualify as abolishing marriage, and that still putting bounds, and enforcing bounds, on marital relationships shows how entrenched the concept of marriage is. 


[^1]: "Reversing" a certification is equivalent to divorce.

[^2]: Anybody can initiate a community report on a marriage, which judges are then obligated to review. Report hearings only take place in local courts (district or county courts). Abuse of community reports can result in this privilege being revoked, which certainly doesn't stop people who are dicks from reporting ex-partners or people they dislike.
